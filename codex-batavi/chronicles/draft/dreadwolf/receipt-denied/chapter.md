# Receipt denied

**Status:** **Draft scratch** — refinement prose; **not** on the Dreadwolf read chain. Does **not** supersede published [*terminus*](../../../dreadwolf/terminus/chapter.md).  
**When:** **168.M42** — Luna Sigma, Noviomagus system (Battle of Sigma Moon), outer storage spine.  
**Unit:** **The Lost** (*Amissi*) — **Lieutenant Einar Dreadwolf**; **Varro** (Judex); **Legatus Armin Sturmwahl** (QVINTA attached, not chain-of-command).  
**Opponent (scratch only):** **Khârn** — mythic upright champion. Published register remains unnamed until rewrite ordered.  
**Beat lock:** [`../../../dreadwolf/terminus/SCAFFOLD.md`](../../../dreadwolf/terminus/SCAFFOLD.md)

Luna Sigma had never learned to be a moon. It had learned to be a ledger — ore crushers, convict bunks, servitor rails, ferrocrete that remembered every invoice stamped into it. When Otho’s *Apex* warning sealed the Warp channels, the page waited for ink that would not come from Administratum quills.

**Armin Sturmwahl** brought **QVINTA** onto the First Vexilla’s outer ring to cut World Eaters geometry before mass learned how to breathe. Forty meters back, **Varro** stood where rear command could still see the spine without pretending to see the Warp. He watched one silhouette the way a gauge watches a furnace: not with hope. With tolerance math.

**Einar Dreadwolf** fought where the corridor widened into storage — Assault Intercessor **Mk X Tacticus** in *Amissi* paint, jump pack for speed more than glory, black pauldrons bearing Chapter wolf and chained wolf-skull **X**, Mephiston Red only on gauntlets and boot feet. Half-helm locked nose and jaw. Mane and **Nails** ran free. **Wolf Talons** scored traitor plate under a red field glow when the **Red Demon** stayed quiet.

When it did not stay quiet, he fought anyway.

The World Eaters arrived as pressure — chainaxe rhythm, boltless charges, Massacre written into skull iron. Batavi answered with short bursts and gladius finishes. No duel. Invoice. Einar’s vector cut through that arithmetic the way Needles cut tide: deny the next purchase before the enemy could name it.

A World Eater champion went down under his left talon. The head came half-loose on ceramite. Brass hunger rose in the air like a receipt printer waking.

Einar crushed the vault with the heel of a traction claw until bone became grit unfit for any throne.

He did not lift it. He did not offer it. He left slag and dust where a bowl might have been, and moved to the next vector as if accounting had already closed the line.

Somewhere behind his teeth, the Red Demon laughed without a mouth.

Somewhere farther than mouths, something vast and brass-colored took note of another unpaid column.

He came upright through his own side’s spray — too clean for the horde’s stumbling liturgy, as if the brass counted only what he collected. Gorechild’s teeth were already wet. The helm that wore amusement like a second face did not bother with the courtesy of a challenge-horn.

**Khârn.**

Einar did not need a slate to file the silhouette. The Nails knew the frequency. The Red Demon surged toward it the way a tide surges toward a moon it has already claimed.

Einar held the chain.

— For the Skull Throne — Khârn said, voice amusement wearing a helmet. — For the Blood God. Give me your head, little wolf. You have been denying my master his due.

Around them, World Eaters and Batavi still died in the spine’s side corridors. The duel did not ask permission from that noise. It made its own pressure — a narrowing that turned ferrocrete into a kill-bowl whether the moon agreed or not.

Einar’s answer was not theater. It was one sentence, tide-flat, QVINTA law bent through *Amissi* watch:

— I deny the skulls to the Red Tyrant.

Then he stopped speaking, because speaking was a luxury the Nails charged interest on, and duty did not pay in speeches.

Khârn laughed as if the denial itself were an offering.

Gorechild came in first — not wild, not wasteful, a butcher’s economy that had ended Legions of better men. Einar met it with Wolf Talons, red field flaring, wrist housings taking the chain-teeth on energized edge. Sparks cut the dust. The impact traveled through ceramite into bone and told both bodies the same lie: *you can end this now.*

Neither ended it.

They traded. Jump-pack burn for a Needles cut past Gorechild’s return; traction claws biting ruptured deck for purchase that should not have held; Khârn’s axe taking a pauldron lip and peeling grey paint into red revelation. Einar’s half-helm rang. Mane and cable-loom whipped. The Nails sang high enough to scrape thought into a single geometry: **close, cut, deny.**

Blood left both of them in honest quantities. Batavi gene-grace. Traitor ichor. It painted the spine in receipts the Throne could have counted if counting were the point.

Counting was not the point.

The point was that neither of them fell when falling was owed.

Einar felt it first as wrong duration — a second that stretched past the math of muscle and implant, past even Exanimus hypertrophy’s ugly surplus. He refused the Red Demon’s hand on the tiller. He refused the hymn rising in the Nails. He refused the part of himself that wanted to take a skull and make the ledger honest by Brass standards.

Each refusal made him angrier.

Each refusal made him stronger in the way a failing bulkhead becomes stronger just before it screams — pressure finding more metal to stress, not more peace to grant. He did not think *gift*. He thought *hold*. He thought *We hold, as the Needles hold the cold tide.* He thought the names of eleven brothers who had died as Batavi before an enemy could bite their souls, and he spent that thought like fuel without asking who had filled the tank.

Khârn felt the unpaid invoice and smiled inside the helm.

— Good — he said, almost gentle. — Bleed longer. The Throne likes a debt collected with interest.

Gorechild took Einar across the ribs. Ceramite cracked. Something warm ran inside the seal. Einar answered by opening Khârn’s thigh plate and making the Betrayer limp for three steps that should have been fatal and were not. They circled in a narrowing that smelled of copper, slag, and incense that had never been holy.

Varro’s gauge, forty meters back, climbed past every tolerance he had filed since Moraine-Nine.

Armin was still cutting World Eaters off the western spur — close enough to hear the duel as a different kind of weather, too far to enter its spacing without becoming another receipt.

Time stopped pretending to be fair.

Einar’s jaw locked harder than Exanimus law had ever asked. Canines pressed the half-helm’s inner plate until the metal tasted like blood from the inside. His shoulders thickened under the Tacticus shell in a way that was not armor flex and not mere adrenaline — mass rewriting itself toward a sentence the Chapter named only in sealed annexes. The chain in his soul geometry — marine visage holding wolf and demon — frayed with an audible-in-the-blood sound that was not sound.

Brink. The edge before the pits’ absolute monster — before a body became a receipt Brass could raise without needing a skull bowl, because the beast itself would be trophy enough. He felt the crossing open like a trapdoor under the atlas pin and refused the drop.

The Red Demon pushed for the crossing.

Einar refused.

The refusal fed the heat again — angrier, denser, lasting past sense — and Khârn pressed into that heat like a man warming his hands at a pyre he had built from someone else’s house.

— Become it — Khârn invited, axe rising for the kind of kill that ends in lifted bone. — Or die as meat. Either way, He is paid.

Einar’s left Wolf Talon buried in the ruptured containment wall behind him as Khârn pinned him — not because Einar had lost the geometry, but because the brink had stolen a half-second of clean vector and the Betrayer had bought that half-second with ninety-four years of unpaid skulls. The extended blades of the right talon hung raised, red field still alive, close enough to Khârn’s throat to promise a mutual ending if either man still believed endings were mutual.

Einar’s thumb found the sealed port at his atlas joint — the place Drusus had trimmed and Varro had watched, the ***Spina Cineris*** he had carried through a century of managed Furor and a forced Exanimus and every watch interval since.

He did not give Khârn the head.

He did not give the Throne the Versibar.

He paid the only denial left that Brass could not reclassify as offering.

A muffled detonation — not wall-breach, not bolt — **inside** the collar seal.

Molecular destabilization unmade the cranial vault without granting the galaxy a bowl to raise. Brain and occipital lattice flashed to ferrous ash and ionized vapor. The second phase — cervical flash inside Noviomagus gasket geometry — welded the neck seal before spray could become liturgy.

Khârn recoiled from a body that collapsed while the half-helm still held.

No skull lifted clean. No jet painted the ferrocrete for Khorne’s accountants. Only grit, slag smell, and a faceplate fogged with dust, not rain.

The brink never became a morph. The debt never became a trophy. Both columns stayed unpaid.

Armin reached the wall too late to strike, too early to pretend ignorance. He saw the ash vent, the port sealed, the brother already gone in every sense that mattered to Brass.

Varro crossed the distance without hurry.

He looked at Khârn once — at the empty hands that had expected trophy weight — then at the collapsed armor. Behind the Executor wolf-skull, the Judex filed what the Throne had wanted and what the watch brother had refused: not only a skull denied, but a completed beast-receipt denied at the last possible millisecond.

— No skull — Varro said, voice flat as a filed edge. — No spill. Hold denied you.

Khârn screamed and came for the Judex. Varro’s *Crozius* answered in Sentence mode; the geometry of that exchange belongs to the forty-two Batavi the moon counted among its dead, not to this scratch.

Angron would descend minutes later. Alaric would walk to meet him. QVINTA would be pulled into evacuation vectors when Furor brothers tried to advance on the Castellan’s duel.

**Einar Dreadwolf** would not be on the stretcher list.

Drusus’s teams would find *Amissi* armor at the storage spine — Mk X Tacticus, *Spina Cineris* pin spent, progenoid tissue annihilated, harvest null. *Vermilion* filing: destroyed by terminis protocol, not by corruption verdict.

On the gunboat bay, with Luna Sigma shrinking on the auspex, Armin would not speak the QVINTA cadence aloud. He would touch fist to chest once — the same salute Einar had given when the cull blade rose at Moraine-Nine — and turn to the next vector.

Varro would close the line:

*In watch. Under the Deathwolf’s jaws. No soul-name. No precedent. No cult.*  
**Terminus 168.M42 — Luna Sigma. Receipt denied.**
